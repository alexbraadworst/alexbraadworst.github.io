## Back to school shopping
I wanna start with how much I just loathe this shit. I was out a day or two ago going out to eat with my bestie and I saw this headline on the wall TV that said something along the lines of "average back to school spending is $850 per student" and some other stuff about how massive the industry is. Genuinely, what could one POSSIBLY be spending this much on????? Not like most students in the US buy their own laptops these days, you can only use school provided ones in most districts I know of.

Oh right, it's clothes. It's ALWAYS clothes. 

I get trying to look cool for the new school year, and I always try to make sure my outfits are all nice and cute and stuff, but holy hell, do we need to go and get a whole new wardrobe every single time that xth number on the report card goes up? I'm sick and tired of trying to shop for stuff I actually need and being blasted with a crowd of 500,000 in the aisles over (used to mostly be girls, but now the guys are also getting into overconsuming...) buying articles of clothing I'll never see them wear. 

That's an overgeneralization, obviously this extends beyond clothes (backpacks, lunchboxes, and water bottles come to mind... usually Owalas or Stanleys...) but either way it's genuinely so bad. How much of this stuff's gonna end up in a landfill next year? Worse still, how much of this is going to sit in a cupboard next year and the years after, never going to someone who could actually use it? I dunno, just my thoughts.

## New school & stuff
My school district follows a model where instead of having several 4-year high schools, there's 9/10 schools that feed into three massive senior high schools. Now there's some benefits to this I know, i.e. the senior high can offer more specialized classes and formats for juniors and seniors, it condenses the budget, it's easier to hire teachers qualified enough to teach advanced courses... etc. That's great and all, but there's a bit of an issue; the student population's massive, and it's currently being renovated.

It really does need a renovation. The current building was built in the late 90s and since then it's not been updated substantially, which means there's worn facilities. Issue is that the school year's already started and they're still not remotely close to being done with it.

For one (and this is a big one), the main stairwell's in the area of construction. We're still attending classes while work is still happening, but that means the entire central part of Building A is walled off as they keep working... that includes the two main stairwells. Since there's also an entire main hallway on the first floor that's also in that area, it means that the entire student population of something like 2,800 people is crammed onto a single staircase that only fits two people across. Obviously not everyone's going up and down that stairwell every period or have classes in Building A... but that is the main building. Our passing periods are 7 minutes and it takes something like 4 minutes just waiting in traffic at that one stairwell. If we had the main staircases, that same walk would've easily taken under 3 minutes in its entirety. Everyone's late to every class basically and there's nothing they can do about it for now. What a cock-up.

Secondly, it seems they're not exactly done even with the bits of the building that are ostensibly done with renovations. My APCSA classroom, just as an example, has a pipe in the ceiling that's sprung a leak of either sewage or something else (think it's sewage). I don't doubt that this is gonna be fixed quickly, but having a mysterious brown mass just drop from the ceiling and miss the rubbish bin to end up on the carpet while we were doing the first lab of the year was a little.... interesting, let's say. I think the leak's already busted a computer in that room that's directly under it. The bathrooms are also a little... interesting, still. The paper dispensers seem to have gone rogue in about half of them, I dunno if they were stolen or if they just haven't been installed, but either way there's no choice but to wipe your hands on your shirt.

Now, there's two high schools that feed into my senior high... and oh man, I can definitely tell who went to my school even if I don't know them, and vice versa. It's about as clean of a split as you can get between the two schools that make up the feeders for my senior high; mine was arguably the most competitive school in the district and the environment was so insanely single-mindedly academic that it seemed like a constant bombardment of cramming and stress. Only think I could really use to describe it's "ppalli-ppalli" if you've heard that term used before. It's the archetypal school of the sweat. The other school's by some metrics the worst 9/10 high school in the district. Not to say mine's better, everyone who went to my 9/10 is depressed as hell and will probably burn out either this year or next year, but damn. First day of school I already saw some people skipping and walking out.
