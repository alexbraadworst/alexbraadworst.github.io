Many people who enjoy and have knowledge about classical music are really unapproachable. I've seen this firsthand more than once, and not just with classical music: the hardcore suck at selling their hobbies to newbies. However, there's another extreme to this: the tendency for people's first exposure to be the same three or four composers (Bach, Mozart, and Beethoven, maybe Vivaldi if you're lucky), or a handful of specific contemporary performers. I'm sure Andre Rieu has encouraged plenty of people to start enjoying classical music, but I don't think that's the impression we should be giving off as members of the space, because he and other people like him just reinforce a perception of classical music being aristocratic - something for rich suburbanites in gala attire to frolic about whimsically to, rather than music for the rest of us to listen to. Not that there's anything wrong with rich suburbanites enjoying his concerts too, everyone ought to be welcome, but that's not exactly the image that draws new listeners in.

What I thought I'd do is present some works from across the western classical repertoire for you, especially if your exposure to classical music so far hasn't been the most flattering. Hopefully you can connect them to your own experiences and find your own joy in them. I speak from experience: when I was younger, all I thought classical music was, was just background noise that played in lobbies, or maybe rolled in the background of adverts for perfumes that cost more than my entire life was worth, or something. Let's all help break down the stigma of classical music in the western tradition being music for the elite, because that just isn't the case.

I'll write my full thoughts and breakdowns of each later, for now you just get the list. Sorry, maybe check back later :(.

Note: Some of these pieces are *long*. If you just want to listen to one movement in particular or whatever, put it on your playlist and don't feel bad about it! If you happen to like just a particular movement of a symphony and don't care for the rest, then you don't need to force yourself to listen to it just because I said it's amazing front to back. That's all your call, and you definitely don't need to learn music theory or whatever to listen to classical music. Enjoy the ride and don't listen to people who knock your personal listening decisions.
